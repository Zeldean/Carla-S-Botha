# CARLA.S.BOTHA
## WEBSITE DESIGN & CONTENT SPECIFICATION

**Website concept:** An eclectic, colourful, interactive art gallery  
**Brand:** Carla.S.Botha  
**Associated educational brand:** C4 Akademie  
**Languages:** Afrikaans and English  
**Overall character:** Creative, playful, personal, colourful, slightly eccentric and professional without feeling corporate.

---

# 1. THE BIG IDEA

Carla.S.Botha is a personal creative brand bringing together three central areas:

1. **Art and creativity**
2. **Mindfulness and wellbeing**
3. **Education through C4 Akademie**

These areas are connected by a shared philosophy: people learn, think, feel and create in different ways. Creativity, confidence, personal growth and emotional wellbeing are important throughout the brand.

The website should feel like **visiting an unusual, colourful art gallery belonging to Carla**.

Rather than navigating through a conventional collection of menu tiles, visitors explore rooms filled with different artworks, objects, frames and signs.

Each major artwork is also an entrance to another area of the website.

The experience should feel personal, imaginative and memorable.

**Central design philosophy:**

*One creative world. Different rooms. Different artworks. Many things to discover.*

The website must be visually exciting without being confusing or difficult to navigate.

# 2. VISUAL IDENTITY AND ATMOSPHERE

## 2.1 Overall aesthetic

The visual direction combines:

- Eclectic gallery walls
- Pop art
- Vintage and antique objects
- Painterly artwork
- Realistic photography
- Collage and mixed media
- Neon signs
- Quirky illustrations
- Colourful educational imagery
- Unusual objects displayed as art
- A touch of maximalism

The different artistic styles are intentional.

This is not a website where every illustration must match.

It should feel as though Carla has collected and created a wonderfully unusual assortment of art over many years.

## 2.2 Colour direction

There is no strict colour palette.

Recurring colours include:

- Hot pink
- Electric and bright blue
- Sunshine yellow
- Purple
- Red
- Green
- Black
- White

Other colours can appear where they suit an individual artwork.

The overall appearance should feel vibrant, but the gallery environment must provide enough visual balance to prevent it from becoming overwhelming.

## 2.3 Gallery surroundings

The artwork should be the focal point.

Gallery spaces can include:

- Distinctive wall colours
- Decorative frames
- Small museum-style information labels
- Quirky wall objects
- Occasional neon lighting
- Vintage decorative details
- Interesting furniture or gallery props
- Subtle shadows and realistic depth

Each main room may have a slightly different atmosphere while still feeling part of the same gallery.

Decorative elements should enhance the experience rather than compete with the clickable artworks.

## 2.4 Artworks and frames

The gallery must NOT consist of identical square pictures.

Different artwork formats should be deliberately mixed:

- Square paintings
- Portrait paintings
- Landscape paintings
- Oval paintings
- Circular artworks
- Unframed artwork
- Ornate antique frames
- Simple modern frames
- Sculptural objects
- Signs
- Neon objects
- Artist palettes
- Collage-like pieces

Some artworks may be ACEO-sized in appearance.

Others should feel much larger.

**An artwork's shape should match the concept.**

For example, a shop sign can actually be shaped like a sign. A painter's palette can appear as an actual palette rather than a picture of one inside a rectangle.

## 2.5 Gallery arrangement

Artwork should appear irregularly arranged, as in a curated gallery wall.

However, the overall composition should feel balanced and intentional.

Every artwork occupies its own invisible square display area, even when the artwork itself is round, narrow, oval or unusually shaped.

This gives different artworks enough breathing room.

Whitespace around smaller pieces is intentional.

The desktop experience should feel like a gallery wall. On phones, artworks should remain large enough to appreciate and select, without losing their individuality.

## 2.6 Gallery labels

A small museum-style label can appear below or beside each interactive artwork.

Labels should identify the artwork's destination clearly.

They may contain:

- Section title
- Optional playful artwork title
- Optional medium or material
- A very short description
- A subtle invitation to explore

The text can be quirky and humorous without making navigation unclear.

Artwork titles and creative descriptions are optional and can be finalised once the individual artworks are complete.

The main identity is **Carla.S.Botha**.

The website represents Carla as an artist, teacher, writer and creative person.

On a larger screen, the gallery should feel like a curated exhibition wall, with different artworks arranged in a balanced but irregular composition.

- Their different shapes
- Their distinct identities
- Their museum labels
- Sufficient space around each artwork
- Clear navigation

The smaller-screen experience must not simply resemble a conventional list of identical buttons.

## 13.6 Readability

Although the visual identity is playful, informative pages must remain readable.

Longer descriptions, price lists and product details should be clearly organised.

The gallery's eccentric personality should not interfere with finding essential information.

# 15. CONTENT AND DESIGN PRINCIPLES

## 15.1 Personality over corporate appearance

This is Carla's personal creative world.

The website should not resemble a generic tutoring company, wellness business or online art shop.

## 15.2 Professional credibility through content

Qualifications, experience and services should be presented clearly.

The overall visual identity can remain quirky and playful.

Professionalism does not require removing personality.

## 15.3 Only display what is ready

Avoid filling the website with unfinished ideas or empty sections.

Potential future areas include:

- Robotics
- SmartyPantZ
- Additional subject-specific resources
- New art courses
- Additional mindfulness products
- Expanded creative services

These can be incorporated when they are ready.

## 15.4 Avoid excessive duplication

Some destinations are deliberately accessible through multiple places.

Examples:

- Mindfulness products from the Mindfulness room and Shop
- Mindfulness downloads from the Mindfulness room and main Freebies section
- Brain profiling from Mindfulness and C4 Akademie
- Artworks for sale from Prints and Paintings and Shop

Visitors should encounter the same underlying information wherever they enter.

## 15.5 Maintain the gallery illusion

Use artwork, labels, framing and spatial composition to make gallery rooms feel distinctive.

Information-heavy pages may be simpler, but should still carry subtle elements of the overall gallery identity.

## 15.6 Respect the mixed artistic styles

Do not force all artworks into one illustration style, one frame design or one colour scheme.

The website should look curated, not standardised.

# 18. OVERALL CREATIVE VISION

Carla.S.Botha should feel like entering the colourful, unconventional world of an artist who also happens to be an experienced teacher, educational psychology graduate, writer and mindfulness enthusiast.

A visitor should be able to wander through the gallery and discover different sides of Carla's world.

A parent looking for mathematics tutoring might discover an artist.

The website should encourage that natural exploration without sacrificing clarity.
